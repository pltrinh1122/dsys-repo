## Amendment 1 — conditional matters (~07:24)

- **Direction:** "pattern covered in general."
- **Change:** new conditional-matter type (§1, S1–S3, §6, §7). Claim form: "*if* disposed, dsys should gain X such that Y, because Z" — designs the map, not the road. S2 runs the discriminating test on the hypothetical; S3 names a conditional source instead of a proposer (drafting ≠ proposing); STOP kills the design, not the idea; new verdicts adopt-the-design / adopt-the-design-with-conditions / refuse(-with-a-map). Invariant: adopting the design never adopts the expansion.

## Amendment 2 — motivation stated (~07:26)

- **Direction:** the operator's question — whether `extend` should cover operator intent and motivation, "what world problem or gap does K3 actually solve?" — then "Y."
- **Change:** new S5 entry conditional "motivation stated": the world problem or capability gap as a concrete scenario (who the current system fails, how), held to S1's anti-vibe bar. Claim form becomes "...such that Y, because Z" (live and conditional). §7 gains the motivation-fit lens: a design holding every conditional while defeating its own Z is refused back not-ready.

## Amendment 3 — Z bites hardest (~07:31)

- **Direction:** "Z bites hardest — i.e. misplaced motivation invalidates the complete extension request."
- **Change:** S5 is load-bearing, not one gate among five: the because-Z is the matter's foundation and part of the claim's identity — a new Z is a new matter, not a reframing. §7 motivation-fit goes two-tier: *fit failure* (Z real, design doesn't serve it → refuse not-ready); *misplaced Z* (gap doesn't exist, or solution-first with post-hoc Z → the complete request is **INVALID**, terminal and distinct from killed — killed may return reframed; invalid returns only as a new matter with new S1–S5 and lineage). KEEP judges misplacement on cited evidence; operator misplacement-findings at disposition are reject.
