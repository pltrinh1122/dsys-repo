## Dialectic

- **Thesis (for clerk):** the name must teach the *cell* in one common
  word. A clerk acts on presented papers without investigating the world:
  stamps, files, disburses, posts. The three vetted referents
  (digest-poster, inbox-organizer, news-synthesizer-poster — DR-CMD-093
  addendum) are all clerk-shaped jobs. The word passes the 12th-grader
  test; its org-function flavor is consonant with DR-CMD-071's label
  theme; and it keeps the disposed scope — the fourth archetype was
  admitted and implemented cell-shaped (CL1–CL6), already green.
- **Antithesis (for line):** the two-axis model (DR-CMD-092) reveals the
  existing names are *pole* names — staff names the acting pole
  (propose-only), office/field name the reading poles — and the may-act
  pole is unnamed. 'Line' completes the staff/line pair (military and
  business usage: line roles do, staff roles advise), yielding staff:line
  :: office:field — the full 2×2 named. 'Clerk' names a cell, breaking
  the pole scheme, and carries drudgery connotations that undersell the
  D4 1.0 authorization weight.
- **Synthesis:** the pole/cell distinction is the crux. 'Line' is the
  right name for the may-act *pole* — but adopting it as such
  restructures the scheme (executor becomes line+field; seven verified
  profiles churned), exceeding the disposed scope ("include our fourth
  archetype," cell-shaped, implemented green). As a mere *cell* name,
  'line' misleads: it evokes the acting pole yet would not cover the
  executor. 'Clerk' names the admitted cell exactly. The drudgery
  connotation is accurate rather than damning: the archetype *is*
  bounded routine action on standing disposition; the authorization
  weight lives in the invariants (CL2/CL4), not the name.
